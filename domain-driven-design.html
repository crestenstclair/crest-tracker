01 · The product model

A musician composes, designs sounds, performs, mixes, curates assets, and preserves the result. The saved result is one Song. These are different responsibilities within one product, not different users or a proposed fleet of services.

The most important modeling distinction is between intended music, reusable sound, the installed sound used by this Song, and what is currently sounding. Each changes for different reasons and obeys different rules.

Confirmed decisions

  • One Song is the sole document. It has one arrangement, up to 16 composition tracks, local Chain/Phrase definitions, library references, and local sound/routing/mix state. There is no Set, parent project, collection of child songs, or selected-song index.
  • New and Open replace the current Song. Dirty content uses Save/Discard/Cancel. Save As writes a separate file containing the same single arrangement.
  • Sounds are shared as definitions. An installed sound use, MIDI subscription, routing, and mix belong to the current Song. Editing a library definition does not share live Patch state.
  • Saved sound revisions remain pinned. A Song adopts a newer revision only through explicit update. Adoption preserves local routing/mix; failure retains the previous pin and configuration.
  • Tracker and Synth remain independent libraries. Synth owns sound generation and stays usable without Tracker. Library boundaries do not establish the complete domain map.
  • Views do not own music. Song, Expanded, Minimap, and phrase presentations project the same composition. A view change must not rewrite notes or restart playback.

What the Song contains

Saved with the Song Referenced from libraries Transient or machine-local
One arrangement; key, scale, tempo, meter Exact reusable sound-definition revisions Playback passes, cursors, generated queues, sounding voices
Chain/Phrase definitions and their structural uses Explicit Custom chord shapes/revisions Temporary parameter claims, previews, pending requests
Installed sound associations and local sound configuration Retained sample/SoundFont assets and exact presets Device connections, prepared graphs, decoded buffers
MIDI subscriptions, routing, baseline mix and audio FX configuration Exact referenced content must remain recoverable Editor focus, display mode, paths and device preferences

The document is a coherent capture of several owners, not one enormous mutable aggregate. There is no extra WorkspaceDocument above Song. Native Synth session data can supply the sound portion without introducing Song, Chain, or Phrase into Synth. Whether referenced assets are embedded or retained externally remains a packaging decision.

Evidence: E1 — stakeholder decisions; E2 — product specification; E5 — native Synth capture.

02 · Where modeling effort belongs

Subdomains describe product capabilities. Bounded contexts describe where a model has a consistent meaning. Neither is a synonym for a folder, screen, thread, or library.

Capability Classification Why it matters / investment
Composition and harmonic authorship Core Reusable musical structures, explicit harmony, varied phrase forms, and safe shared editing are central product behavior. Invest in expressive rules and predictable edits.
Musical sequencing and performance Core Independent occurrences, precise musical time, MIDI FX, and cleanup obligations turn authored intent into a performance. Invest in deterministic traces and timing contracts.
Playable sound design Core candidate Synth is a first-class product: engines, presets, envelopes, mappings, and sound-shaping effects. Its market differentiation is not established by code size; reuse upstream DSP.
Mixing and audio-effect arrangement Supporting Routing, independent stems, summed tracks, shared returns, mute/solo, and baseline controls require exact behavior.
Sound-asset curation Supporting Import, retain, identify, inspect, audition, and assign usable material without silently changing identity.
Sound execution Supporting Admit, sustain, release, and render voices with explicit resource limits and activation outcomes. Hard real-time engineering is important but does not alone make a core domain.
Device connectivity Supporting Maintain usable physical connections and reject stale ingress. Reuse driver and protocol mechanisms.
Song preservation Supporting responsibility Recover the exact composition and sound, including pinned resources and a truthful dirty baseline. Serialization and filesystem operations are generic mechanisms.

Use existing parsers, codecs, DSP engines, effect implementations, operating-system dialogs, and device libraries. No new domain is justified merely to wrap one of them.

03 · Proposed bounded contexts

Nine candidates describe the whole product. This is a proposed semantic map, not a claim that today's Synth already has nine clean modules. The ninth candidate—Song Preservation—may be satisfied by application orchestration. Device Connectivity may also remain a cohesive host module. Neither needs a dedicated service or an extra document type.

C01 · Composition Authoring

Proposed context Core music

Purpose: preserve musical intent and the consequences of reuse.

Owns: Song arrangement, composition tracks, Chain/Phrase definitions, structural uses, note and FX data, generator inputs, and shared/unique editing rules. Definitions and incoming references are scoped to this Song.

Does not own: active playback passes, physical devices, sound-engine settings, or sounding voices.

Boundary test: changing a shared Phrase changes all its uses. Make Unique isolates only the selected occurrence, including its containing Chain when needed. The complete current reference topology must be checked at commit.

Rationale: editing an intention and executing it have different lifecycles. There is no evidence for separate Arrangement, Phrase, or Generator bounded contexts yet; forms resolve to a common event contract.

E2 · E3

C02 · Harmony

Proposed context Core music

Purpose: preserve explicit pitch and chord meaning while helping the musician choose material.

Owns: scale and interval rules, literal chord shapes, voicing, harmonic queries, the derived read-only Default bank, and editable Custom chord definitions.

Does not own: arrangement order, transport state, instrument voices, or a competing note array for interval display.

Boundary test: choosing C major in a minor Song still yields C–E–G. A scale change may refresh suggestions; it cannot silently turn a selected major chord into minor.

Rationale: a reusable editable vocabulary and contextual derivation have a lifecycle beyond one phrase edit. A module within Composition remains a credible simpler implementation; do not split chord math and chord storage into invented contexts.

E2

C03 · Musical Performance

Proposed context Core performance

Purpose: turn musical intent into timed actions.

Owns: performance runs, occurrence passes, exact musical time, tempo and flow, FX evaluation, generated scheduling, note-release obligations, and the musical initiation/expiry of temporary control claims.

Does not own: persistent mix baselines, authoritative sound-parameter arbitration, DSP, or actual voice allocation.

Boundary test: two tracks can reuse one Phrase and progress independently. Stop ends future scheduling and initiates cleanup; it does not prove every external destination is silent.

Rationale: a structural use can be traversed many times. A live execution pass is not a Phrase field, and a scheduled note is not a sounding voice.

E2 · E6

C04 · Sound Configuration

Proposed context Synth sound model

Purpose: define and install a playable sound.

Owns: reusable sound definitions, engine capabilities, exact preset/asset assignment, envelope, Drum Rack mapping, ordered sound-shaping post-FX, and musical voice configuration. Installed uses carry their pinned definition association, accepted configuration, and local subscription. This owner resolves baseline/effective values for its sound parameters.

Does not own: asset custody, inter-source routing/mix, actual voice lifetime, or automatic global updates.

Boundary test: saving a valid library definition requires no audio activation. Installing a structural revision requires a matching activation acknowledgement before committing the accepted installation.

Rationale: a shared recipe and a Song-local installed Patch have different identities and lifecycles. Reuse a canonical sound-configuration value; do not introduce a second independently editable copy of Synth state.

E1 · E4 · E8

C05 · Sound Asset Library

Proposed context Resource custody

Purpose: retain and identify usable sound material independently of its assignment.

Owns: retained sample/SoundFont content, import validation, library identity, asset metadata, preset coordinates, and availability queries. Both instruments and effects can reference retained material.

Does not own: installing a Patch configuration, graph activation, or audition playback.

Boundary test: an import can succeed even when a later assignment fails. A SoundFont preset is identified within its exact bank asset by bank/program coordinates, not by its display label.

Rationale: material can exist unassigned, be reused, and outlive its external source file. Existing library-relative locators are not evidence of globally immutable content identity; revision retention remains a design task.

E7

C06 · Mixing

Proposed context Synth mix model

Purpose: combine sound contributions predictably within the Song.

Owns: source routes and trim, source sends, independent mixer tracks, summed-track sends, shared returns and their ordered effects, master controls, saved mix baselines, and effective mix-control resolution.

Does not own: reusable sound-definition content, composition tracks, note generation, or DSP algorithms.

Boundary test: a Patch send processes an individual stem; a track send processes the sum. A return processes the incoming sum once. Mute wins over solo. Expiring a temporary send reveals the latest manual baseline, not a captured old value.

Rationale: empty mixer tracks and shared returns have meaning without a one-to-one instrument relationship. Current Patch route/trim/sends remain canonical Synth fields; this is a proposed ownership seam, not permission to move them or bypass the reducer.

E9 · E5

C07 · Sound Execution

Proposed context Synth runtime

Purpose: realize admitted sound with the active configuration.

Owns: active sound generation, voice admission/sustain/release, transient audition, admitted compatible controls, resource exhaustion, and activation outcomes.

Does not own: Song traversal, asset retention, library publication, or baseline authorship.

Boundary test: a requested configuration, an activated graph, an accepted configuration commit, and an audibly heard result are different facts. Failure cannot silently substitute another engine, asset, or preset.

Rationale: actual sounding state differs from desired configuration and musical obligations. DSP processing and the real-time callback belong here as implementation mechanisms; they are not separate contexts merely because they run on another thread.

E10 · E8

C08 · Device Connectivity

Boundary candidate Host / devices

Purpose: maintain eligible physical connections and explicit recovery state.

Owns: device identity, selected preference, candidate/active connection revision, availability, negotiated capabilities, and recovery requests.

Does not own: an intended note target, Patch subscription, musical sequence, or direct mutation of voice state.

Boundary test: stale input from a retired connection is rejected before it reaches the current sound state. Disconnection initiates cleanup; it does not imply physical cleanup succeeded.

Rationale: physical identity and connection lifetime differ. A cohesive host module may be sufficient until independent model or policy complexity justifies stronger separation. There is no separate Destination Management context established by the evidence.

E11

C09 · Song Preservation

Boundary unresolved Document lifecycle

Purpose: preserve and restore one coherent Song with exact referenced sound.

Owns as a responsibility: capture completeness, reference continuity, replacement eligibility, save outcome, and the exact captured clean baseline. File identity and pending continuations are application lifecycle metadata.

Does not own: another musical container, child-song membership, resource internals, device connections, playback passes, or sounding voices.

Boundary test: a successful save of revision 10 cannot mark edits from revision 11 clean. Failed Open retains the current Song; a missing pinned sound never falls back to latest.

Rationale: this adapts the earlier preservation proposal to the user's single-Song decision. A separate bounded context is not yet justified over a Song lifecycle coordinator using owner-valid captures. Keep that decision open; do not recreate Workspace or Set as a root.

E1 · E5 · E3

Boundaries deliberately not invented

No context per engine, effect slot, phrase form, screen, repository, or worker. Authoring and Harmony may share implementation packaging. Sound Configuration and Mixing could also remain in one canonical control module while their rules remain explicit. None of these choices requires microservices, a message broker, event sourcing, CQRS, an ORM, or a new domain framework.

04 · Context relationships

Observed current terrain

Synth has a mixed canonical control model: Patch contains sound configuration, envelope, post-FX, subscription, routing, sends, and voice settings; mixer tracks are independently persistent. AppState::apply is the sole product-state mutation path. The host coordinates workers, prepared graphs, activation acknowledgements, and saved captures. Tracker remains a plan and design repository, not an implemented runtime.

Proposed translations and authority

Boundary What crosses Who remains authoritative
Composition ↔ Harmony Effective key/scale, literal chord values, selected shape revisions, derived suggestions Composition owns its selection and source notes; Harmony owns harmonic rules. Neither reads another owner's mutable internals.
Composition → Musical Performance Validated definitions, structural-use identities, immutable revisions, generator inputs Authoring owns intended content; Performance owns its current execution passes and adoption timing.
Musical Performance → Synth ingress Specific installed-sound target or intentional endpoint/channel message, musical controls, release obligations Host translation preserves target meaning; Synth admits messages through existing control paths. A musical event is not a direct voice call.
Sound Configuration ↔ Asset Library Exact asset identity, kind, metadata, preset coordinates, assignment facts Library owns retained material; Configuration owns whether a given assignment is valid.
Configuration / Mixing → Sound Execution Prepared configuration and effective controls; matching activation and failure observations return Parameter owners own accepted baselines and effective meaning; Execution owns actual active generation and voice behavior.
Musical Performance → parameter owner Addressed temporary claim, lifetime, expiry/cancel Performance owns musical claim lifetime. Mixing resolves sends; Configuration resolves its sound parameters. Execution applies the admitted result.
Device Connectivity ↔ host Current eligible connection revision, availability and capabilities Connectivity owns connection eligibility; the host combines it with intended target and current subscriptions.
Song Preservation ↔ content owners Owner-valid captures, exact resource references, reconstruction results Each owner validates its own content. The coordinator preserves and replaces one coherent Song.

These are model relationships, not organizational claims. No evidence establishes separate teams, a Partnership, or Customer/Supplier planning. The Tracker-to-Synth translation can serve as an anticorruption boundary where it protects musical meaning from Patch/channel/runtime details; ordinary serialization alone does not earn that label. MIDI is an interchange protocol, but current evidence does not establish a complete host mixer/send control profile.

06 · Invariants and consistency boundaries

An aggregate protects a true consistency rule. The fact that objects share a file does not require them to share an aggregate. These are candidate tactical boundaries, to be implemented with existing records, functions, collections, and reducer operations where sufficient.

Rule Required behavior Candidate owner / consistency
R01 · One document New/Open replace the active Song; Save As never adds an arrangement. Failed or cancelled replacement preserves current content. Song lifecycle coordinator; validated replacement publication, with Synth activation semantics respected. No parent aggregate.
R02 · Safe isolation Make Unique changes exactly the selected occurrence. Validate path, source revisions and complete current incoming-reference topology together. Song/Chain/Phrase authoring owners; one serialized atomic copies-and-relinks transaction.
R03 · Independent playback Reused content has independent cursor, RNG, repeat and FX memory per execution occurrence. Views do not mutate it. ActivePerformance runtime owner; definitions remain free of runtime state.
R04 · Literal harmony Default is derived/read-only. Custom edits preserve valid explicit shapes. Selected chords retain their intended pitches and quality. CustomChord root plus immutable harmonic values and pure derivation. No mutable Default aggregate.
R05 · Pinned reuse Library publication never updates the Song implicitly. Explicit adoption preserves local subscription, routing/mix and unrelated pins. SoundDefinition revision and InstalledSoundUse association; distinct publication and adoption operations.
R06 · Acknowledged installation A structural candidate commits only after its matching activation acknowledgement. Stale or failed results cannot replace accepted sound. Existing Synth structural coordination plus the relevant installed configuration/mix owner.
R07 · Faithful assets Retain validated content before publishing its identity. Assignment and audition are separate. Restore exact preset identity or report failure. RetainedAsset can be a simple immutable record. Assignment belongs to Configuration; preview belongs to Execution.
R08 · Correct mix Preserve stem-versus-sum sends, one processing pass per return, ordered effects, mute/solo and tap semantics. One coherent local mix candidate referencing installed sound uses; no repository per send or fader.
R09 · Temporary means temporary Claim expiry recomputes from the latest baseline. Claims and voices are excluded from saved capture. Mixing or Configuration owns parameter resolution; Performance owns musical claim lifetime. Exact overlap/recovery policy is still open.
R10 · Exact save baseline Only the successfully written capture becomes clean. Later edits remain dirty; a pending replacement must protect them. Application save/capture lifecycle using owner-valid immutable snapshots.
R11 · Honest delivery Queued MIDI does not prove admission, audible playback, or silence. Release obligations survive relevant target changes. Musical Performance, host ingress and Sound Execution each report their own outcome. No blind MIDI retry.
R12 · Current connection Admit only the acknowledged connection revision; disable obsolete ingress before recovery. DeviceBinding runtime state and the existing connection lifecycle.

Smallest useful tactical model

  • Song, Chain, Phrase: identity-bearing authored roots with outgoing structural uses. A normal edit updates its natural owner. Make Unique is a justified cross-root atomic operation; unrelated edits do not require a giant composition-graph aggregate.
  • CustomChord: an editable definition with literal shape and voicing values. Default banks are replaceable query results.
  • SoundDefinition: identity plus exact retained content revisions. A valid library save has no requirement to produce sound.
  • InstalledSoundUse: Song-local association with a pinned definition and accepted configuration. Reuse the canonical configuration value. Local persistent sound overrides are not assumed by this proposal.
  • Local mix: routes, sources, tracks, returns, master and baseline/effective rules. In standalone Synth, the same semantics are native session-local; Synth does not gain a Tracker-specific SongMix type.
  • RetainedAsset: immutable retained content and metadata may suffice; a catalog is not automatically a large mutable aggregate.
  • ActivePerformance / ActiveSoundExecution / DeviceBinding: distinct runtime consistency owners. Individual voices, UI rows, decoded buffers, and meter samples do not need repositories.

Useful values include exact musical durations, typed pitch/interval/chord shapes, a specific-sound versus endpoint/channel target, validated parameter values, and scoped revision identities. Prefer meaningful values over a wrapper class for every primitive. A row position, graph revision, library path, or display label is not a substitute for the identity a workflow actually needs.

Why Make Unique needs more than optimistic source versions

Suppose a Phrase isolation preview appears safe. Before commit, another edit adds a new incoming use of the containing Chain within this Song. The selected path and Phrase revision might be unchanged, yet the isolation consequences changed. Commit must validate the authoritative topology and recompute the plan; a changed preview fails without partial writes. This is the retained earlier Tracker skeptic correction, narrowed to the current Song.

07 · Representative workflows

Each trace includes its failure boundary.
W01 · Edit a shared Phrase or make one occurrence unique

Intent: edit music while choosing the scope of the change.

  1. Input becomes a semantic edit command containing occurrence identity, expected source/path revisions, and shared/unique intention.
  2. Composition obtains authoritative incoming references. The owner validates the edit; a pure isolation function proposes required copies and relinks.
  3. A serialized authoring commit rechecks versions and topology. Copies, relinks, result and any session request deduplication commit together; undo is one operation.
  4. Projectors read the accepted version. Performance may adopt a new realization at its defined boundary; that policy does not alter the editing transaction.

Reject: stale or ambiguous occurrence, changed isolation consequences, invalid content, or a reused request ID with another payload. No partial copies or relinks. Edit Shared intentionally affects every use in this Song.

Queries: where is this definition used, what will be copied, which revision is currently executing?

W02 · Publish a sound revision, then explicitly adopt it

Intent: improve a reusable sound without changing existing music unexpectedly.

  1. Sound Configuration validates and retains a new library definition revision using exact required assets. Publication leaves the current Song's pin unchanged.
  2. The musician explicitly chooses a revision for a particular installed use. Resolve exact content and build a candidate using this use's local addressing and preserved mix.
  3. Validate compatibility. A structural change uses Synth's preparation, preflight, activation and matching acknowledgement lifecycle through the canonical reducer.
  4. Only the accepted installation updates the committed association and marks the Song dirty. Other pins stay unchanged. Saving the Song captures the adopted association and local state.

Reject: missing retained revision or asset, unsupported configuration, stale expected revision, or failed/mismatched activation. Keep the previous pin, accepted configuration and mix. No global live propagation, automatic latest-on-open, or cross-document transaction is implied.

Queries: which revision is pinned, which newer revisions exist, what failed during adoption? Retention/deletion and graph-swap claim/tail policies still require decisions.

W03 · Import, assign, activate, and audition an asset

Intent: retain useful material, hear it, and deliberately assign it to a sound.

  1. The asset adapter validates kind/content and retains a usable library resource off the audio callback.
  2. A correlated result identifies the originating request. Configuration validates the assignment against current origin, request and graph/configuration state.
  3. A worker prepares the graph; the existing control path preflights and admits it. Matching activation acknowledgement commits the installed configuration.
  4. Audition is a separate transient operation through the origin's sound/mix path. It does not assign the asset or change the saved document.

Reject: invalid content, wrong capability, missing exact preset, stale origin or late completion. A successful import survives a failed assignment. Never retry an uncorrelated installation or replace a missing preset with a nearby one.

W04 · Temporary send, manual mix edit, and Save

Intent: automate a temporary sound change while preserving authored mix.

  1. Performance initiates an addressed claim with a musical lifetime. Mixing owns the send baseline and resolves the effective value.
  2. While a send is temporarily 35%, the musician changes the baseline from 20% to 25%. The active claim still determines the effective value under the chosen policy.
  3. Save captures the 25% baseline, not the temporary 35%. When the claim expires, resolution exposes 25%, never the old 20%.
  4. Stop/seek cancels or expires owned claims through the agreed cleanup contract. Sound Execution applies admitted effective controls.

Implementation gate: current ordinary Synth send setters change fields captured by SavedSession. That path does not prove the proposed baseline/effective overlay exists. Exact overlap precedence, external unknown baselines, reconnect and graph-swap compatibility remain open. Do not implement this as “write a send, then restore the old value.”

W05 · Save, then replace the current Song

Intent: preserve work and open or create one replacement document.

  1. Capture owner-valid authored content, exact resource pins, installed sound state and mix baselines. Exclude runtime claims, voices, prepared graphs, devices and editor state.
  2. A persistence worker writes the immutable capture. Only correlated success updates file identity and the clean baseline; later edits remain dirty.
  3. Dirty New/Open uses Save/Discard/Cancel. Cancel or failed prerequisite Save leaves the current Song intact. Protect any edits made after capture before continuing replacement, either by a validated admission gate or by checking the current revision again.
  4. Read/migrate/validate/prepare the candidate before publishing it. Coordinate native Synth activation and accepted content replacement; failure preserves the prior Song and usable sound state.

No new container: the coordinator has lifecycle state, not a collection of Songs. The native Synth replacement workflow is reusable evidence, not proof that a complete Tracker Song adapter already exists.

W06 · Perform, stop, and survive connection changes

Intent: play the intended notes and discharge their release obligations.

  1. Performance validates content/target capability and starts independent execution passes on one musical timeline.
  2. The host translates a specific installed sound into Patch-targeted normalized MIDI, or intentionally uses endpoint/channel delivery. Channel delivery fans out to current subscribers.
  3. Sound Execution admits actual voices independently of the sequencer's intended note obligations. A queue acknowledgement does not prove a sound was heard.
  4. Stop cuts off future scheduling and starts owned cleanup. Lost connections disable stale ingress and request recovery; status distinguishes pending, failed and completed work.

Open counterexample: changing a Patch subscription between channel Note On and Note Off can change the receiving set. Current fanout alone does not guarantee release of the original recipients. Capture/resolve release ownership and graph/device generation semantics before promising safe cleanup. Never blindly retry a potentially delivered Note On.

09 · Integration with the existing product

Reuse what is already demonstrated

  • Canonical Synth mutation: keep AppState::apply, existing AppLoop ingress, correlated effects and read projections. Multiple semantic contexts do not create multiple mutable copies or bypass routes.
  • Targeted MIDI: AppEvent::Midi { patch_id, message } supports normalized Patch-targeted ingress. dispatch_midi_from separately implements intentional channel fanout. Translate user channels 1–16 to internal 0–15 at the boundary.
  • Graph lifecycle: reuse off-thread preparation, preflight, block-boundary activation, acknowledgement and off-thread retirement. A library-definition save is separate from installation.
  • Native capture: current SavedSession schema version is 4. It captures configuration, subscriptions, routing and mix, and excludes device/runtime/editor state. An older “version 2 JSON” sentence remains in the Synth design reference; current source is the schema evidence.
  • Resource capabilities: use existing engine/effect/asset/device ports, typed failure, exact presets and upstream implementations. Existing hard-coded Synth capacities are implementation debt, not new product requirements. Tracker's 16 composition tracks and three musical FX slots are separate confirmed limits.

Capabilities that must not be assumed

Gap Evidence / risk Smallest proof before implementation
Precise scheduling ingress Inspected AudioCommand::PatchMidi has no timestamp. Queue draining before rendering does not prove sample-offset scheduling. Trace or implement one timestamped scheduling contract and measure its jitter/latency under the real render path.
Temporary baseline/effective controls Ordinary send setters modify persisted Patch/mixer fields. Demonstrate temporary claim + concurrent manual edit + save + expiry without changing the saved baseline accidentally.
Portable sound revisions Current Patch identity is installed identity; a library-relative asset locator is not a retained content revision. Define exact definition/revision identity and restore one pinned old sound after publication of a new revision.
Generic host MIDI controls Engine CC behavior is not proof of mixer/send/patch lifecycle coverage. Audit the supported control profile, units, targeting and unsupported outcomes. Keep lifecycle/resource operations distinct from live musical control.
Owned release after target changes Channel fanout resolves current subscribers per message. Exercise subscription change, graph replacement and device loss between Note On and release.
Complete Song replacement Native Synth has guarded session replacement; Tracker Song persistence is unbuilt. Prove one captured Song restores composition, exact sound pins and local mix; failed preparation leaves the prior document usable.

Incremental path, without a speculative rewrite

  1. Keep the two library APIs usable. Record semantic ownership around existing canonical Synth operations before considering field moves.
  2. Prove a playable Phrase slice through current MIDI ingress, with common events, three FX slots, occurrence-owned state and deterministic capture.
  3. Implement shared authoring/isolation and explicit harmonic values before adding more views or independent models.
  4. Introduce portable definition revisions and installed-use associations at the reuse boundary. Preserve native sound types and local mix.
  5. Add owner-scoped transient control resolution only after the baseline/save contract is proved.
  6. Build one-Song capture/replacement around existing persistence and activation behavior. Validate old revision recovery and dirty-state safety before widening scope.

This report is a design input for Tracker and the host. Synth's current DESIGN.md remains its as-built reference. No Synth code or specification is replaced by this document.
